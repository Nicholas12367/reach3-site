## (d) Copy Patterns to Mirror or Avoid

### Mirror (these patterns work for Lloyd buyers)
1. **Lead with the border-town identity.** Reid & Wright: "built our reputation on the border of Alberta and Saskatchewan". Octopus: "unique border town". This resonates locally — use phrases like "Alberta-Saskatchewan border", "Border City", "AB/SK".
2. **Quantify reach in body copy.** Sign Advantage and Big Sign both lead with traffic numbers ("63,319 daily views", "up to 65,000 views per day", "13,000 households"). Reach Screens should publish total monthly impressions across the network on the homepage.
3. **"Stand out" / "eye-catching" / "high-impact" language.** Magnetsigns: "Stand Out". Sign Advantage: "EYE CATCHING". Onsite: "eye-catching signs". This vocab is expected — use it but pair with proof.
4. **Service trinity in tagline.** Y's Marketing: "Marketing • Advertising • Promotional". Octopus/Red Bicycle use similar triplets. Reach Screens tagline could echo: "Digital Signage • Local Screens • Real Results".
5. **Local + national playbook simultaneously.** Onsite and Magnetsigns rank because the parent brand has authority and the page is geo-specific. Reach Screens should have one geo page per city in the network (Lloydminster, Vermilion, Wainwright, Bonnyville, etc.) — programmatic done well.
6. **Vertical landing pages.** Kobzza shows this works ("Why Choose a Marketing Partner That Specializes in Home Services?"). Build one page per vertical: restaurants, gyms, salons, dental, retail, auto.

### Avoid
1. **Vague "creative agency" boilerplate.** Red Bicycle and Octopus rank on agency-name brand only — their pages are full of self-referential fluff ("Let's Ride Together", "we want to be your sidekicks") that says nothing about services or geography. Reach Screens should be specific and outcome-focused.
2. **Title-tag city-stuffing.** Reid & Wright's title lists 4 cities, which dilutes Lloydminster ranking. Keep title tags Lloydminster-first.
3. **No meta descriptions.** Every single competitor scanned was missing a meta description. This is a free win — write strong 155-char meta descriptions on every page.
4. **HTTP / no-HTTPS sites.** Sign Advantage's site is still HTTP. Reach Screens already has TLS — a small but real ranking signal.
5. **Programmatic city-page spam.** ICS Creative and Kobzza use thin "[City Name] Digital Marketing Agency" pages with auto-generated copy. Google increasingly penalizes this; don't copy the pattern, but DO build genuine local pages with unique content per city.
6. **Burying the value prop in brand language.** MADE leads with "stop struggling & start soaring" — pretty, but a Lloyd small-business owner Googling "advertise my restaurant" won't connect. Use plain-language headers.
7. **Facebook-only presence.** SignSpecial has no website. Direct competitor weakness — Reach Screens should aim to own every signs-related SERP they could've competed for.

## Notable surprises from the scan

1. **Almost zero meta descriptions across the entire competitive set.** Twelve competitor pages scanned, zero had a discoverable meta description. This is universal SEO neglect — Reach Screens can leapfrog every local rival with one afternoon of meta-description writing.
2. **The Lloyd "digital advertising" SERP is dominated by content-farm directories from India (Moris Media, Moris PR, ConvertMate, Guru SEO Services).** No genuine local player ranks for "digital marketing agency Lloydminster" outside of programmatic out-of-town agencies. The local market is wide open for a real Lloyd-based digital-signage operator with proper SEO.
3. **No competitor owns the "screens inside venues" framing.** Every player is either outdoor (billboards) or sign-shop (vinyl/portable). Reach Screens' indoor-network value prop has no direct keyword competition — it's a genuine blue ocean for terms like "in-store advertising Lloydminster", "waiting room TV ads", "restaurant screen advertising".
4. **Sign Advantage owns Lloyd's only commercial digital billboard but their site has no HTTPS and almost no on-page SEO.** A local outdoor competitor with strong physical assets and weak digital — beatable on SERPs without competing on physical placements.
5. **"Lloyd" (the nickname) is used in copy by Reid & Wright but appears in zero title tags or H1s.** Worth experimenting with it as a long-tail signal.
